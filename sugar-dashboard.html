The market in one page

The permanent structure. Refresh yearly.

The ethanol hinge — sugar's defining mechanism

Brazilian mills crush cane into sugar or ethanol, and can swing the mix roughly 5–10 percentage points season to season — several million tonnes of world supply — based on the relative price of sugar vs ethanol (itself driven by gasoline, crude and Brazilian fuel policy). Consequence: sugar has a crude-oil beta no other soft has. A crude rally is sugar-bullish (cane diverts to ethanol); a crude collapse is sugar-bearish with a lag. The UNICA fortnightly mix number is the world's swing supply, published every two weeks.

Three exporters, one swing state

Brazil (~21% of production, ~45% of exports) sets the world price at the margin. India is the #2 producer but trades as a policy variable, not a market: export bans/quotas, minimum prices, and an aggressive ethanol-blending program that structurally diverts cane. Thailand is the #2 exporter, monsoon-dependent. The EU beet complex, China and the protected US market matter regionally; the world price is made by the tropical three.

Demand

Steady ~1–2%/yr growth, EM-driven and price-inelastic in the short run. The GLP-1/anti-sugar narrative is a real long-horizon question for Western demand but has not yet dented world consumption growth — Asia and Africa dominate the increments.

Two crushes, two calendars

Center-South Brazil crushes April–December — UNICA reports crush, mix and cane quality (ATR) every fortnight, making Brazil the best-instrumented crop in softs. India and Thailand crush November–April, their cane made or broken by the June–September monsoon. So the market's attention rotates: monsoon watch (Jun–Sep) → Asian crush reveal (Nov–Apr) → Brazilian crush and mix (Apr–Dec) → repeat.

El Niño's split personality here

Unlike cocoa and coffee, El Niño cuts both ways in sugar: it typically means drought risk in India and Thailand (bullish) but excess rain in Center-South Brazil — which disrupts the current harvest and dilutes cane quality (bullish short-term) while recharging soil moisture for the next cane cycle (bearish 27/28). Sugar's El Niño trade is a spread across origins and seasons, not a single direction.

Two contracts, one margin

No.11 (raw, FOB Brazil-centric) vs No.5 (white, London): the white premium is the world refining margin and a stress gauge for the destination markets. Raws are the macro trade; whites carry the EU/logistics stories.

Standing policy & structural variables

India: the state is the market

Export quotas and bans, minimum support prices, and the ethanol blending program (structurally diverting cane toward E20+ targets) make Indian supply a political output. The policy logic is consistent: domestic food-price stability first, exports only from proven surplus. Read the state's behaviour, not its statements — an export quota granted is a bullish-crop signal; import-duty cuts are the confession of a short one.

Brazil: fuel policy is sugar policy

Ethanol demand — hence the mix — depends on gasoline pricing, blend mandates and tax treatment. Any Petrobras pricing shift or federal fuel-tax change is a sugar-supply event in disguise.

The EU's structural retreat

Two loss-making beet years, regulatory pressure on neonicotinoids and inputs, and a ~7.5% area contraction have turned the EU from swing producer to structural importer — the IPR suspension vote and Mercosur quotas now govern how the gap gets filled. Slow-moving, but it's a permanent leg under the world balance.

Demand's long shadow

GLP-1 drugs and sugar taxes are the decade-scale bear thesis on Western consumption. Nothing in world aggregates yet — EM growth dominates — but it's the standing question under every multi-year bull case, worth an annual check rather than a weekly one.

How to listen (no accounts needed)

Bias corrections for this market

Opposite polarity to West Africa: Brazilian producers are fully price-exposed and hold inventory, so they have every incentive to talk the crop down — treat producer pessimism like US-farmer crop talk (de-mean it, demand verifiable specifics). The honest tell is behaviour, not words: Safras' selling-pace percentages and Cecafé export volumes reveal what farmers actually believe. With BRL strengthening (~5.12), that behaviour is currently to hold rather than sell — a real signal, not talk. For florada photos, apply the against-interest rule: a beautiful bloom photo is bearish for the poster's own old-crop inventory — pride posting against the book, hence credible. Read the photo stream, not individual photos: aggregate timing, density and uniformity across regions vs the seasonal baseline is the signal no selection bias can fake. Vietnamese farmer chatter is thin in English; export pace vs crop estimates is the readable proxy for their hoarding.

How this market trades

The crowding warning is the headline. Funds hold multi-year net-long highs into a deficit story that is forecast, not yet measured. That's the inverse of cocoa's clean-short setup: here, good news is partly spent, and a merely-normal monsoon finish or a mix reversal meets a leveraged long with an air pocket underneath. If you take the bull side, you're late to the positioning even if right on the fundamentals — structure and timing matter more than the view. The contrarian setup (fading the premium) has the positioning tailwind but fights three genuinely deteriorating supply legs — it wants a trigger (a fired 🔴), not anticipation.

Liquidity is the best in softs — sugar's open interest and depth dwarf cocoa's, tail risk is lower, and spreads are tight. This is the soft where enter-and-stop campaigns execute cleanest and where futures (not dealer products) are unambiguously the venue.

The structure trades

  • Crude-sugar relationship: the ethanol parity gives sugar an energy leg — a crude view can be expressed or hedged through the mix mechanism.
  • White premium (No.5 − No.11): the refining margin; EU retreat and IPR politics live here.
  • Calendar spreads (Oct/Mar, Mar/May): the Brazilian harvest-tail and Asian-crush timing stories express in the front spreads before flat price.
  • Cross-soft relative value: sugar (crowded long, premium built) vs coffee (clean positioning, premium missing) on the same El Niño driver is the season's most interesting relative-value observation — one weather event, two opposite pricings.

The bull case, honestly

  • Three independent deficit legs: Brazilian ethanol diversion, EU −19%, El Niño over the Asian crushers.
  • Forecast revisions unanimous in direction; Green Pool's −3.2 MMT tail would rhyme with 2023/24 (28 ¢).
  • India importing would be the regime confirmation — the #2 producer turning buyer.
  • Low world stocks after the 24/25 deficit; limited buffer if the tail lands.

The bear case, honestly

  • Record fund long into an unconfirmed deficit — the fear is owned, with leverage.
  • ISO's own number (−0.26 MMT) is a rounding error on a 180 MMT market.
  • Crude weakness flips the Brazilian mix back toward sugar within a season.
  • El Niño is good for Brazil's next cane cycle — the 27/28 supply response is already germinating.
  • A normal monsoon finish deflates the Indian scare entirely.
