The market in one page

The permanent structure. This section shouldn't need refreshing more than once a year.

Supply is a duopoly with a slow clock

Côte d'Ivoire produces roughly 2.0–2.2 Mt in a normal year (~40–45% of world supply), Ghana historically 700–900 kt (~12–15%). Behind them: Ecuador (the fast-growing #3, high-yield CCN-51 hybrid, plantation-style), Cameroon, Nigeria, Indonesia (declining), Brazil. World crop ≈ 4.5–5 Mt.

Cocoa trees take 3–5 years from planting to meaningful yield. That single fact explains the market's multi-year cycles: a price spike cannot summon supply for half a decade, and a price collapse doesn't remove trees already in the ground. Supply responds to prices from years ago, not today's.

West African production is smallholder — millions of 2–5 hectare farms, aging trees, minimal irrigation or inputs. There is no US-style "planting decision" each spring; the supply variable is how existing trees respond to weather, disease and farmgate incentives.

Two harvests per season

The season runs Oct–Sep (2026/27 exceptionally opened 1 Sep). The main crop (Oct–Mar) is ~75–80% of output; the mid-crop (Apr–Aug/Sep) is smaller and lower grade. The critical biological window is cherelle formation (baby pods) roughly May–Aug for the following main crop — weather then determines the harvest six months later. That's why markets trade August rainfall so hard.

Prices are set by boards, not just markets

Neither Ivorian nor Ghanaian farmers see world prices directly. Côte d'Ivoire's Conseil du Café-Cacao (CCC) and Ghana's COCOBOD forward-sell most of the crop and fix a guaranteed farmgate price each season (plus a $400/t "living income differential" premium since 2019). Consequences: farmgate incentives lag world prices badly, smuggling flows across borders when the two countries' prices diverge, and board finances (especially COCOBOD's chronic funding strain) are a genuine supply variable.

Demand = grindings

Nobody measures chocolate bars; demand is proxied by quarterly grinding volumes — beans processed into butter/liquor/powder — reported for Europe (ECA), North America (NCA) and Asia (CAA). Grind is slow-moving (habit-driven consumption) but it did visibly crack after the 2024 price explosion: reformulation, shrinkflation, substitution. Origin grinding (Côte d'Ivoire now vies with the Netherlands as world #1 processor) is structurally taking share from Europe.

Two contracts, one arb

NY (USD) and London (GBP) differ in deliverable growths and quality premia; the NY–London spread widens when the market discriminates between fine/mainstream and bulk supply, and it's a standing structured-relationship trade of exactly the kind you run elsewhere.

The current storylive · 24 Sep 2026

Where we are in the cycle and what the market is actually arguing about.

You are entering this market mid-whiplash, with the tape now consolidating. The 2023–24 super-spike (disease + failed harvests + harmattan damage) took NY cocoa to a record near $12,900/t in December 2024. Recovery supply then crushed it: prices collapsed roughly 75% to a low around $2,846 in April 2026. From there NY rallied more than 100% into August, printed a run to $6,500+, and has since drifted back into the $5,900–6,100 zone as of 11 September (Dec contract ~$5,961). The market is now sitting inside a range instead of trending — and range-bound cocoa is exactly the setup where the 26/27 supply signals are being tested against the two-year-high stocks.

The argument is present-vs-future, and the "future" side keeps adding. The present is comfortable: 2025/26 closed as a recovery year — Ivorian port arrivals reached 2.11 MMT through 2 August, +20% y/y and the highest since 2022/23; Ghana harvested ~750 kt, +25%; ICE certified stocks sit at ~3.41 M bags, a two-year high (727,526 bags as of 1 Sep, flat); ICCO's revised 24/25 balance shows a modest 37 kt surplus. The future is where the flow deteriorates: Reuters pod-counter survey pegs Ivory Coast 26/27 main crop at 1.35–1.45 MMT (>10% below current), COCOBOD flags Ghana at 470–620 kt (a 16–38% drop) with a licensed-buyer view even lower ("more than a third"), CCC's 8–10 week delay is now visible in the projected sub-15,000 t weekly deliveries for September, and El Niño (ONI +1.80) is by consensus about to strengthen the Dec–Feb harmattan.

Positioning is still the amplifier — and it's still leaning the "wrong" way. MM net short has covered from the −19,885 April record to −4,750 contracts on the 8 Sep COT (longs 26,056 vs shorts 30,806) — roughly 76% covered but not yet flipped long. Open interest has drifted lower again, so rallies remain short-covering-driven rather than new-money accumulation. The pre-registered 🔴 "MM flips long" regime-line has not fired; it is still the single most important line on this dashboard.

Demand is the quiet bear case, still unchallenged. European Q2 2026 grindings printed 316,366 t, −4.6% y/y and −2.9% q/q — demand has not recovered even with beans 50% off the highs, and manufacturers are still working through high-cost inventory and reformulated recipes. Origin grind (CI +7.5% cumulative) remains the exception. Next real referendum: Q3 ECA release tentatively 15 October — another −4/−5% print emboldens the surplus camp and cements the range; a positive surprise removes the main bear leg.

The policy news of the fortnight: farmgate divergence is back. Ivory Coast held its guaranteed farmgate at 1,200 CFA/kg for 26/27 (1 Sep). On 9 September Bloomberg reported Ghana intends to lift its farmgate ~6% to 2,737 cedis / ~$240 per 64-kg bag, pending finance-minister sign-off. That reopens the same cross-border wedge the July harmonisation deal was meant to close — Ghana is now the higher-paying side, which pulls Ivorian beans into COCOBOD's system and further degrades the CI arrivals series without changing tonnage. Read together with COCOBOD's own 16%-decline warning and the "more-than-a-third" whispers, and the Ghana view has quietly moved from "structural despair" to "structural despair with a policy tailwind for reported purchases".

The Q4 logistics squeeze is still the special situation. A delayed main crop plus EUDR taking effect at year-end means exporters are compressing shipments into a narrow window — the CCC itself flags port-congestion risk at Abidjan and San Pedro, with something like 900 kt of arrivals potentially landing in Nov–Dec. Ghana adds its own stress: reported six-month payment arrears and ~330 kt of delayed shipments. Logistics stories like this move nearby spreads before they move flat price — Dec–Mar and NY–London remain the natural expressions.

The 2026/27 balance sheetlive · 24 Sep 2026

Combining the accumulated surplus with next season's crop problems: the question is not "surplus or deficit" but "how big is the cushion, and does the trajectory flip." Update focus of this refresh: the two-crop cycle at the mid-to-main transition — port arrivals through the transition and the pod-count read into the main harvest.

The mid-to-main-crop transition, in the actual numbers

The 2025/26 mid-crop closed strong — cumulative Ivorian port arrivals reached 2.11 MMT by 2 August (+20% y/y), a mid-crop that lingered so long into July that it directly caused the CCC's 8–10 week delay warning for the 2026/27 main crop. The mid-crop over-performing and the main crop starting late are not two stories; they are the same story of a delayed, wet biological cycle. The market therefore has to read the mid-crop's strength as pull-forward, not durable comfort. Behind that reading:

  • Ivory Coast — 2026/27 main crop. Reuters pod-counter/exporter survey (July): 1.35–1.45 MMT, vs ~1.6 MMT this year (>10% below). CCC's own 8–10 week delay warning implies weekly deliveries stay below 15,000 t through September and 25,000 t through October — the pace-vs-average extrapolation the market normally relies on is structurally broken until end-October. Farmgate held at 1,200 CFA/kg (1 Sep) — flat at a $6k world price is a slow-burn supply negative.
  • Ghana — 2026/27 main crop. COCOBOD's July range 450–550 kt, softened to a ~650 kt central case (16% decline), and now Bloomberg (3 Sep) cites a licensed buyer at "more than a third" — putting Ghana back in the tail zone. Add Ghana's proposed 6% farmgate hike to 2,737 cedis / ~$240 per 64-kg bag (9 Sep, pending finance-minister approval): moves Ghana above CI in per-kg farmer receipts and would pull unrecorded cross-border beans into COCOBOD's system — good for reported Ghana purchases, bad for reported Ivorian arrivals.
  • Port congestion — Nov–Dec 2026. ~900 kt of arrivals compressed into a Nov–Dec window because of the delayed start plus EUDR go-live, per CCC — the physical bottleneck that turns "late" into "unshippable" if it verifies.

The synthesis

One recovery year rebuilt the cushion only partially: from the 27% crisis low to roughly 28% at end-24/25, heading toward the low-to-mid 30s after 2025/26's surplus — still below the ~35–40% historically associated with a comfortable market. The combined picture is a half-rebuilt buffer meeting a shrinking, delayed flow. Three regimes fall out of it:

  • Consensus lands (+80–150 kt): stocks build a little further, S/G reaches the mid-30s, the market stays structurally comfortable. Rallies driven by crop scares fade; the $5.5–6.5k range holds or erodes. This is what BMI's forecast profile implies.
  • Modest deficit (−50 to −150 kt): the cushion absorbs it arithmetically — this is not 2024, which required years of cumulative drawdown. But markets price trajectory, not levels: the first deficit year restarts the depletion clock and shifts the regime from sell-rallies to buy-dips, because everyone can count forward to 2027/28.
  • Tail (Guan Chong −300 to −400 kt): Ghana at "more than a third" down, CI's 1.35 MMT survey verified, El Niño delivers a real Dec–Feb harmattan, and demand keeps recovering. S/G heads back below 30% within a single season and the 2024 playbook is live again — with a market that is structurally less liquid than it was then. This scenario was a tail in July; the September prints have moved it toward a plausible base.

The underpriced variable is demand, not supply. Every surplus forecast assumes demand stays broken. But combined Q2-2026 grindings across Europe, Asia and North America printed +6.1% y/y (650,671 t) — Europe alone was −4.6%, meaning Asia and North America grew hard (caveats: NA reporting panel changed, Asian grind partly reflects processing relocation). If cheap beans are genuinely reviving consumption at ~2–3%/yr while West African supply contracts ~10%, the projected surplus disappears on the demand line alone. The bear case needs demand to stay dead at half the old price; Q2 is the first evidence it might not. The Q3 ECA print (tentatively 15 Oct) is the referendum.

Honest uncertainty note: BMI and Hedgepoint disagree on the current season's surplus by 117 kt, ICCO is withholding its estimate, Guan Chong sees an outright deficit where StoneX/Hedgepoint/BMI still see a surplus, and Ghana's own official forecast has a 150 kt band on it. The dispersion — now roughly −400 to +150 kt on a 4.7 Mt market, ~12% of the market — is itself the signal: nobody actually knows, which is why weekly arrivals and quarterly grindings will keep repricing the curve violently in both directions.

Standing policy & structural variables

Slow variables that set the stage. Refresh quarterly.

EUDR — the 2026 wildcard

The EU Deforestation Regulation requires geolocation-verified, deforestation-free sourcing for cocoa entering the EU — the destination for the bulk of West African beans. After repeated delays it takes effect for large operators at end-2026, and it's already distorting physical flows (the Q4 shipping compression above). Structural effects to expect: a compliance premium on traceable beans, smallholder exclusion risk, and a possible two-tier market — EU-compliant vs. rest-of-world cocoa.

Origin power is rising

In July 2026 Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Nigeria and Cameroon jointly declared intent to restrict raw bean exports and expand local processing. Take the direction seriously even if timelines slip: origin grind capacity keeps growing (CI ~750 kt capacity, vying with the Netherlands for #1), which gradually shifts what "exports" even means and supports the countries' pricing leverage.

Ghana's structural decay

The bear case on Ghana is not one bad season: swollen shoot virus (incurable — infected trees must be uprooted), decades-old tree stock, and galamsey (illegal gold mining physically consuming farmland) are ratchets, not cycles. Add COCOBOD's chronic financing strain (currently: reported six-month farmer payment arrears) and Ghana's capacity to surprise on the upside shrinks every year. The gap left is being filled by Ecuador — the structural share-shift trade of the decade in this market.

The farmgate wedge

Whenever the fixed farmgate price diverges from world prices or from the neighbour's price, beans move: cross-border smuggling (Ghana↔CI↔Togo/Guinea), under-declared volumes, quality shortcuts. The new harmonized-price arrangement between CI and Ghana is an attempt to close that wedge — whether it holds is itself worth watching.

How to listen (no accounts needed)

The Reuters farmer dispatch — a weekly report built from actual farmer interviews across Ivorian growing regions — is the closest thing cocoa has to AgTwitter, pre-verified and in English. Free via Reuters commodity news and reproduced widely. ADM Investor Services and similar broker dailies aggregate arrivals, grind and positioning with trade-level commentary. CocoaIntel and comparable specialist sites track futures microstructure (open interest, EFPs, certified stocks) daily. The genuinely early stuff — pod counts from specific départements, disease photos, cooperative-level payment complaints — circulates in French on Facebook groups and WhatsApp/Telegram among Ivorian farmers and pisteurs; it is real but effectively inaccessible systematically, which is precisely why the cocoa-2023 style edge existed for the handful of origin-connected accounts that surfaced it.

Bias corrections for this market

Same de-meaning logic as US farmers, different biases: origin officials talk their book upward before farmgate price negotiations (a big crop weakens the case for higher farmgate prices — so boards often lowball early estimates); exporters exaggerate logistics problems they want the regulator to solve; farmers have less price incentive to spin than US farmers (they get the fixed farmgate price regardless) — which actually makes West African farmer weather reports more trustworthy than Iowa crop talk. The COCOBOD pod-count revisions are the exception to official optimism: a board forecasting its own 40% decline is speaking against interest, which is why the market took the July warning seriously.

How this market trades

Microstructure notes that matter for execution and sizing.

Liquidity is structurally impaired post-2024. The price explosion forced margin requirements up and drove commercial hedgers and funds out; open interest never recovered. Thin books + high margins = exaggerated moves, stop-runs, and gap risk. Size accordingly — cocoa at ~$6,000 with today's volatility is a different animal per contract than the pre-2024 market at $2,500.

Positioning is the primary short-horizon driver. The April→August round trip was a positioning event wearing a fundamentals costume: record shorts met deteriorating crop news. The COT is therefore your single most useful weekly read — the same crowding logic as the silver monitor, and cocoa is arguably even more squeeze-prone than silver right now given the depth problem.

  • Open interest still soft (~200k area, drifting lower): the rally has been position liquidation plus tentative new longs, not broad new-money accumulation. A genuine flip of managed money to net long would mark a regime change — the 🔴 pre-registered trigger, not the day-to-day wiggle.
  • Context — the ag complex has cooled off the record. Late-August's record ag-longs build has moderated in early September; sugar and cotton positioning has come off multi-year highs. Cocoa is no longer as conspicuous a laggard — which cuts both ways: less catch-up-longs fuel, less correlated-washout risk.
  • Recurring source: CFTC COT (Fridays, disaggregated futures-only); Saxo's weekly "COT on forex and commodities" note covers cocoa — already inside your broker's research.

The structure trades: NY–London spread (quality/origin discrimination, currency), calendar spreads (the Q4 congestion story lives in Dec–Mar, not flat price), and options — cocoa vol is rich but the gap risk is real, which fits your rule of using listed options specifically for gap scenarios.

The bull case, honestly

  • 2026/27 supply deteriorating: Ghana −13% (or worse), Ivorian main crop late, El Niño overhead.
  • Structural: swollen shoot + aging trees + galamsey = Ghana's decline is a ratchet.
  • EUDR compresses and complicates physical supply into Q4.
  • Funds still carry shorts — fuel above the market.
  • Tree clock: no meaningful new supply (ex-Ecuador) until late decade.

The bear case, honestly

  • Demand is broken: Europe grind −4.6% even after a 50% price collapse; reformulation is sticky.
  • ICE stocks at two-year highs; 2025/26 delivered a surplus and arrivals beat the 5-yr average.
  • Ecuador and other origins are the supply response, arriving now, every year, compounding.
  • El Niño and the pod-count fears are already partly in the price after a +100% rally.
  • The delay story may be timing, not tonnage — beans arriving late still arrive.
