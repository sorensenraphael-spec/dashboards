Energy Playbook — Crude · Diesel · Gasoline

2026 in one line live · 14 Sep 2026

The regime: February's surplus (fastest inventory builds since 2020, Brent under $70) was vaporized in March by the Iran war — the largest supply disruption in oil history: >13 mb/d of exports lost at the peak, Hormuz loadings down from 20+ to ~3.8 mb/d, global supply −10.1 mb/d in a month, dated Brent touching $140. The 17 June US–Iran MOU to reopen the Strait crushed prices to $72; since July the deal has been fraying. Friday 12 Sep settled with WTI ~$100, Brent ~$104 — the rally paused as Iranian state media flagged Tehran meeting Gulf states in Oman over the Strait, a tentative de-escalation headline against a still-degraded flow picture. OFAC keeps the Strait-toll authority sanctioned; the June General License X for Iranian oil sits alongside GL-134 easing Russian-oil delivery — the sanctions map is now product-supportive rather than punitive. UAE remains outside OPEC; OPEC+ ratified its Sep +188 kb/d hike (the last of the 2023-cut rollback) and on 6 Sep held Oct quotas flat, calling out infrastructure-attack risk in the communiqué.

The tension to trade: physical reality (record global draws, ~5.1 mb/d in Q2) vs. the banks fading it — EIA STEO (9 Sep) lifted its Brent path to a ~$90 average through 2H26 and $91 full-year; JPM's fade case at $80 Q4 is now the outlier. Every headline cycle since June has resolved as: escalation spikes sold, de-escalation dips bought. The Oman-track talks are today's dip-buy trigger for the fade camp — but STEO also flags US distillates falling below 100 M bbl in October for the first time since 2003, so the fade has to survive a physical winter, not just a diplomatic weekend.

Book — open transatlantic diesel spread

Structure: short US Heating Oil Oct-26 (24,000 gal @ ~423.8 avg) vs long ICE UK Gas Oil Oct-26 (120 @ ~1,269.6). One thesis, two legs: Europe's winter is tighter than America's — EU pays up relative to US as heating season lands on record-low European stocks with Gulf exports still impaired.

  • What pays it: EU cold snap, EU gas-storage shortfall spilling into gasoil heating demand, further Gulf/Red Sea disruption of eastern supply to Europe, US export surge to EU (drains US but is priced in HO weakness vs LSGO strength).
  • What kills it: a US Northeast cold snap into the lowest US distillate stocks since the mid-90s — the short leg is the squeeze-prone leg; a mild EU winter; Hormuz durable reopening (compresses everything, hits the long leg harder).
  • Climate edge (verified): ONI JJA at +1.80 — strong El Niño winters tilt the US Northeast warm (bearish HO demand, supports the short leg) and typically suppress late-season Atlantic hurricanes (fewer Gulf refinery hits). The European El Niño signal is weak/mixed — the long leg gets no climate help, but needs none if EU stocks stay this low.
  • Leg integrity rule: if either leg gets stopped or force-closed, the survivor is a naked position that must re-justify itself within the hour — resize, re-hedge or close. No repeat of the silver amputation.
  • Roll calendar: Oct contracts — diary the broker's expiry cutoffs now; roll ≥10 days before. The exit date is never again the broker's choice.

Honest flag: both legs sit at record-crack levels — this is a relative-value position built at absolute extremes. Size for the scenario where the whole complex normalizes at once.

Bull / bear ledger

Bull (complex, distillate-led)

  • Record global inventory draws; OECD on-land stocks fell ~146M bbl in a single month at the war's peak
  • US distillates at ~30-year seasonal lows into heating season + harvest
  • EU double deficit: diesel lowest since 2022 and gas storage ~50% — fuel-switching bid both ways
  • No spare refining kit; every closure since 2020 is now a bid on cracks
  • Hormuz still restricted; each escalation re-prices the whole curve in hours
  • OPEC cohesion broken (UAE exit) — spare capacity less deliverable than quoted

Bear / normalization

  • A durable Hormuz reopening returns >10 mb/d of flow capability — the single biggest bearish binary in any market you trade
  • Demand destruction is real: JPM's case rests on it; China showed imports can be cut hard
  • $90+ cracks are their own cure — every refinery on earth is running for diesel; supply response compounds
  • El Niño warm-winter tilt in the US removes the cold-snap kicker from HO
  • Gasoline already oversupplied — the byproduct glut caps total refinery economics eventually
  • SPR / IEA coordinated releases remain a policy option at $100+
