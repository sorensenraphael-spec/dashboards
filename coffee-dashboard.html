The market in one page

The permanent structure. Refresh yearly at most.

Two species, two exchanges, one spread

Arabica (NY, ~55–60% of world supply): higher quality, higher altitude, frost- and drought-sensitive. Brazil dominates, then Colombia, Ethiopia, Honduras. Robusta (London): hardier, more caffeine, instant-coffee and espresso-blend workhorse. Vietnam is the giant, Brazil's conilon second, then Indonesia and Uganda. Roasters substitute between them at the margin, so the arabica–robusta spread is a real economic dial, not just a chart — when arabica gets expensive, blends quietly get more robusta, and vice versa.

The biennial cycle — coffee's master rhythm

Arabica trees alternate on-years and off-years: a heavy crop exhausts the tree, the next year yields less. Brazil's whole national output breathes in this two-year rhythm, and every balance forecast starts from which phase Brazil is in. 2026/27 is a record on-year; 2027/28 is the biennial down-leg — which is why an El Niño arriving exactly now is taken so seriously: it would stack a weather shock on top of a biological down-year.

The weather windows

Flowering (florada), Sep–Nov: the first spring rains after Brazil's dry winter trigger the blossom; failed or staggered rains mean fewer, uneven cherries — the next crop's ceiling is set in these weeks. Frost, Jun–Aug: the classic tail event (July 2021 → the 2022 spike); one cold night can kill years of tree growth. El Niño actually reduces frost risk (warmer winters) while raising heat/drought risk — it trades one tail for another.

Free market, currency-driven

Unlike cocoa's West African boards, Brazil is a free market: farmers and co-ops are fully price-exposed, sell when they choose, and hold inventory as a trading position. Consequence one: the BRL matters enormously — producers earn dollars and pay costs in reais, so a weak real triggers origin selling and caps rallies. Consequence two: origin selling behaviour is a live sentiment indicator — Brazilian farmers hoard into strength and dump into weakness like any trader.

Demand: the steadiest in commodities

Coffee consumption grows ~1.5–2%/yr through almost anything — habit-driven, caffeine-anchored, with China and Asia compounding on top. It did not crack under record 2025 prices the way cocoa demand did; retail pass-through is slow and drinkers grumble but pour another cup. Demand is the boring, reliable line; virtually all the action in coffee balances is supply.

Structurally thin balance

World ending stocks run around 25 M bags against ~165 M of use — roughly 8 weeks of consumption, versus cocoa's ~15. Coffee lives permanently closer to the edge, which is why single weather events move it 15% in a day and why the frost/florada calendar is traded so hard.

The current storylive · 7 Sep 2026

Where we are in the cycle and what the market is arguing about.

Same shape as cocoa, faster clock: a comfortable present against a feared future. The route here: five consecutive underperforming Brazilian arabica crops plus tight stocks drove NY to a record above 437 ¢/lb in October 2025. Then the record 2026/27 crop came into view — USDA pegs Brazil at 71.9 M bags (+14%) and world output at a record 189.7 M bags (+6%) — and the market collapsed ~45% to ~240 ¢ in early June. What followed was the object lesson in coffee microstructure: a rain-delayed, quality-impaired harvest (64% done mid-July vs 77% a year earlier), El Niño headlines and the tariff-driven certified-stock drain produced a +16% single-day move on July 6 and a ~46% rally in weeks — most of which has now faded back to ~293 ¢ as the harvest finishes (~85–90% by end-August).

The tension is in the stocks, not the crop. A record harvest is arriving, yet certified arabica stocks sit at a 2.5-year low (~243k bags) — because the 50% US tariff on Brazilian coffee rerouted flows (US buyers voiding Brazilian contracts and substituting other origins), exporters held back, and the delayed harvest slowed replenishment. Robusta certs are meanwhile at a 5.25-month high with Vietnam shipping a 4-year-high crop. So the two exchanges are telling opposite stories — nearby arabica tightness inside an aggregate surplus — and the arabica–robusta arb plus NY calendar spreads are where that contradiction gets traded.

The forward fight is 2027/28, and it's being decided this month. The bull case stacks three things on the same season: the biennial down-year, an El Niño the CPC calls potentially the strongest in 75+ years hitting the Sep–Oct florada, and Espírito Santo heat risk through January's bean-filling. The bear counterweights: producer irrigation build-out since 2021 has genuinely raised resilience, El Niño suppresses the frost tail, Vietnam's robusta machine is running at full output, and Rabobank sees a 9.5 M bag arabica surplus in 26/27 rebuilding the cushion first. The market's honest position: the record crop is real, the threat to the next one is real, and September's rainfall maps will pick the winner.

The synthesis

Even the record year only lifts ending stocks to ~26 M bags — one bad season's worth of buffer. So the scenario tree is compressed: a decent florada means two consecutive rebuild years and a price grind back toward the pre-spike range (the June low at ~240 marks that world); a failed florada means the record crop's surplus gets consumed the moment it exists, and with stocks this thin the market goes back through 350–400+ faster than it came down. Coffee never earns cocoa's "comfortable" zone — the cushion argument that caps cocoa's tail simply doesn't exist here, which is why coffee's convexity is the fattest in softs.

Uncertainty note: USDA, CONAB and private forecasters routinely disagree on Brazil by 5+ M bags (different survey methods), and the tariff makes trade-flow data temporarily unreliable as a demand proxy. CONAB's third 2026 survey (September) and the first 27/28 estimates (January) are the anchors to watch.

Standing policy & structural variables

Slow variables. Refresh quarterly.

The US–Brazil tariff wedge

The 50% tariff on Brazilian coffee imports (2025, still in force) hit a flow that supplied roughly a third of US green coffee. Effects running through the system: US buyers substituting Colombia and other origins (their differentials firm), Brazilian coffee rerouting to Europe and Asia at discounts, certified stocks draining as an alternative US supply source, and US retail coffee inflation. Any negotiation headline is an instant repricing event across certs, diffs and the KC curve.

EUDR — same wall as cocoa

Coffee is in scope of the EU Deforestation Regulation taking effect for large operators at end-2026: geolocation-verified, deforestation-free sourcing into the EU. Brazil's big co-ops are relatively well prepared; the compliance pain concentrates in smallholder origins — Ethiopia, parts of Vietnam, Uganda — where a two-tier (compliant vs non-compliant) market and diverted flows are the likely outcome.

No boards, but a central bank

Brazil has no cocoa-style marketing board — the state variable is the BRL. Real weakness = origin selling pressure on rallies; real strength = farmers hold. Watching USD/BRL alongside KC is not optional; it's half the price action on quiet days.

Vietnam's quiet transformation

Record export revenues are financing replanting, irrigation and a shift of some acreage to durian and other fruit — robusta supply is reliable now but the acreage competition is a slow structural headwind worth an annual check.

Origin & social chatterlive · 7 Sep 2026

What the ground-level conversation says, and how to listen.

Current themes in the chatter

  • "September decides everything" — trade and origin commentary has converged on the florada as the sole topic; the current crop is old news before it's even fully shipped.
  • El Niño superlatives everywhere ("strongest in 75 years", "super El Niño") — same drumbeat as cocoa, running ahead of verified anomaly data. The co-op voices are notably calmer than the headlines: resilience up via irrigation, heat the real fear, too early to quantify.
  • Quality complaints from the rain-delayed harvest — chatter about cup quality and screen sizes from the 26/27 crop supports the "record quantity, scarcer quality" nuance behind the certified-stock draw.
  • Tariff workaround talk — rerouting, triangulation, differential games. When the physical trade discusses logistics tricks, watch differentials rather than flat price.

How to listen (no accounts needed)

StoneX's free daily coffee report is the single best listening post — COT detail, spreads, EFP flow, origin-selling colour, in trade language. Safras & Mercado and Cooxupé's harvest/selling-pace updates give the Brazilian producer's revealed behaviour. Reuters' Brazil coffee dispatches and co-op president quotes (Expocacer, Cooabriel) carry the ground voice. And uniquely to coffee: the florada is photographed — Brazilian producers flood social media with flowering images every Sep–Nov, a literal visual crowd-census of the next crop that requires no account to browse via news aggregation.

Bias corrections for this market

Opposite polarity to West Africa: Brazilian producers are fully price-exposed and hold inventory, so they have every incentive to talk the crop down — treat producer pessimism like US-farmer crop talk (de-mean it, demand verifiable specifics). The honest tell is behaviour, not words: Safras' selling-pace percentages and Cecafé export volumes reveal what farmers actually believe. For florada photos, apply the against-interest rule: a beautiful bloom photo is bearish for the poster's own old-crop inventory — pride posting against the book, hence credible — while flowering complaints align with the book and get discounted. And read the photo stream, not photos: aggregate timing, density and uniformity across regions vs the seasonal baseline is the signal no individual selection bias can fake; individual images inherit all the usual selection and virality contamination. Co-op agronomists sit in between — quotable, informed, mildly long-biased. Vietnamese farmer chatter is thin in English; export pace vs crop estimates is the readable proxy for their hoarding.

How this market trades

Microstructure notes for execution and sizing.

The most violent market in softs. July 6's +16% day was not an anomaly — coffee does this every few years (frost 2021, the 2024–25 squeezes). Thin books, high margins, weather binaries and a structurally thin stock cushion make gap risk permanent. Size for 15% days; this is the market where your rule of using listed options for gap scenarios applies most literally.

Positioning is currently mid-range, not extreme. Managed money's last sourced arabica read is ~+25k net long (May) — neither the crowded long of late 2025 (which fueled the Feb–Jun collapse) nor short. The washout already happened; June's low liquidated the old bull consensus. That means the florada verdict lands on relatively clean positioning — moves from here are more likely to be fundamental than positional, unlike cocoa where the short base still frames everything. Refresh the COT before acting on this.

The structure trades

  • Arabica–robusta arb: the quality-substitution dial — currently stretched by the certified-stock divergence (arabica draining, robusta building).
  • KC calendar spreads: delivery-pressure games around notice periods are a recurring feature (the Mar/May and Jul/Sep episodes this year); the front spread is where nearby tightness expresses first.
  • KC vs BRL: half of coffee's quiet-day price action is currency. A short-BRL rally is origin selling waiting to happen.
  • Physical differentials: Colombia/other-origin diffs firming = the tariff rerouting in real time, often ahead of futures.

The bull case, honestly

  • 27/28 stacks three shocks: biennial down-year + strongest-in-decades El Niño at flowering + heat through bean-filling.
  • Ending stocks ~8 weeks of use even after a record year — no cushion exists to absorb a failed florada.
  • Certified arabica stocks at 2.5-year lows; nearby tightness inside the surplus.
  • Positioning cleaned out — the June washout removed the crowded long; fresh fundamental buying has room.
  • Demand never breaks: ~2%/yr through everything, China compounding.

The bear case, honestly

  • A record 189.7 M bag world crop is arriving now; Rabobank sees a 9.5 M bag arabica surplus rebuilding stocks.
  • Irrigation build-out has genuinely raised El Niño resilience — the 23/24 episode cut Brazil ~8%, not 30%.
  • El Niño kills the frost tail — the classic bull catalyst is off the table for 2027.
  • Vietnam at a 4-year-high crop with surging exports; robusta comfortable.
  • A normal florada makes this a two-year rebuild story: the June ~240 low is the gravity well.

What 47 years of data established (pack 20260907)

The fade is dead: unconditional fade hit-rate 57% (not the folkloric 8/10), avg fade-loss 18.0% vs avg gain 12.7% → EV ≈ 0. ENSO conditioning is suggestive, not significant (EN mean +3.75% vs −1.23%, Welch t = 0.88; bootstrap CI −4.9/+12.3). Entry level is the strongest conditioner (t ≈ 1.8): expensive-tercile entries mean −6.3%, median −8.4%, P(<−15%) = 38% — and 2026 enters at the 88th percentile. The EN∩expensive cell (the 2026 configuration): 4 of 5 historical losers. Skew flips with level: avg win/loss 22.7/−5.2 in mid years, 13.4/−20.6 in expensive years. Timing: Sep mean −1.8%, Oct–Nov +2.1%, avg trough Oct 4; in this window weakness beyond ~8% is information, not opportunity (deeper dips → worse outcomes, monotonic, robust across 47 years). Short futures rejected on path: median MAE for Sep-1 shorts in expensive years +9.3%, 62% exceed +8% — enter-and-stop conversion would fail even in winning years.
