The reference frame

Not education — the machinery list, so refreshes are mechanical.

The report calendar (the densest in commodities)

Monthly: WASDE (~10-12th) — the referee for everything. Weekly: crop progress & conditions (Mon), export inspections (Mon), export sales (Thu), CFTC COT (Fri). Quarterly: Grain Stocks (the ambush report — largest surprise variance of any USDA release) and, annually, Prospective Plantings (31 Mar) and June Acreage. Daily: USDA flash sales announcements (the "daily reporting system" — 100kt+ sales same-day). South America: CONAB monthly, Rosario & Buenos Aires exchange weeklies. Black Sea: SovEcon/IKAR/UkrAgroConsult.

The hemispheric mirror

The complex never sleeps: US crop made May–Sep, priced into harvest (now); South American crop planted Sep–Dec, made Dec–Feb (soy) and Feb–May (safrinha corn), priced against the US old crop. Every northern autumn, attention hands off to SA planting weather — that handoff is happening this month, into an El Niño whose classic signature (wet southern Brazil/Argentina) is supply-friendly.

The structures you already trade in spirit

Board crush (beans vs meal+oil) — the processing margin, currently policy-inflated on the oil side via biodiesel. Old/new crop spreads (Dec/Jul corn, Nov/Jul beans) — where "this crop vs next crop" trades explicitly, the natural home of the SA-planting view. Wheat class spreads (KC−Chicago protein premium, Minneapolis spring) and Matif−CBOT (the Black Sea stress gauge in EUR). Corn−wheat feed substitution.

Equity-book cross-links (unique to this dashboard)

Biofuels policy → FutureFuel, Delek: RVO/45Z headlines move soyoil and the biodiesel margin stack — the same alert serves both books. the monthly Purdue Ag Economy Barometer is the standing capex-sentiment gauge; record grain prices now = the 2027 equipment/irrigation spending impulse. Ethanol grind → corn demand and the refining-adjacent names.

The current storylive · 23 Sep 2026

Where we are in the cycle and what the market is arguing about.

The tape is fading a florada it hasn't verified. KCZ26 closed 284.25 ¢/lb on 11 September, a fresh 10-week low and −16.4% over 30 days, on favourable near-term GFS runs for Brazil and Rabobank's call for arabica to fall roughly another third by year-end. Two sessions later Somar reported zero rain in Minas Gerais for the week ended 13 September — a total reversal of the 8.9mm / 127%-of-average priming print of 30 August that set the "flowering-friendly" narrative in the first place. The tape and the ground are moving in opposite directions for the first time this window, and that is the setup the florada playbook was written for.

The tension is in the stocks, not the crop. The record 2026/27 crop has arrived — Brazil finished harvest at a realized ~75 M bags (StoneX/Sucafina/Marex, above USDA's 71.9 M June print) and USDA still projects world output at a record 189.7 M bags (+6%). Yet certified arabica stocks have ground down to ~223,900 bags — the lowest since 1999, undercutting the 226k mark flagged only a week ago. The 50% US tariff on Brazilian coffee is the persistent driver: US buyers substituting Colombia and other origins, exporters holding back Brazilian flow, and pending gradings not building the refill sequence. Robusta certs meanwhile sit near multi-month highs with Vietnam projected at a record 32.5 M bag crop. The two exchanges continue to tell opposite stories — nearby arabica tightness inside an aggregate surplus.

The forward fight is 2027/28, and September has just delivered its first hostile datapoint. The bull case stacks the biennial down-year, a now-verified strong El Niño (ONI +1.80) hitting the Sep–Oct florada, and Espírito Santo heat risk through January bean-filling. The 13 Sep zero-rain print is exactly the shape of the failure trigger — one week is not a verdict, but the moisture debt widens with every dry week, and the 🔴 "no meaningful widespread rains by ~10 October" line is now under three weeks away. Bear counterweights remain live: irrigation resilience (23/24 El Niño cut Brazil only ~8%), the frost tail suppressed by El Niño, Vietnam robusta comfortable, Rabobank's 9.5 M-bag arabica surplus rebuilding stocks first, and the Colombia offset already failing — the FNC now projects Colombia at only 12.5 M bags after climate-driven flowering losses of its own, which tightens rather than loosens the tariff-substitute chain. Positioning has cleaned to +22.1k managed money net long (8 Sep COT), so any conviction move lands on mid-range books.

Standing policy & structural variables

Biofuels: the demand policy lever

US RVO volumes and the 45Z clean-fuel credit set the marginal demand for corn (ethanol) and soyoil (biodiesel/renewable diesel). This is the standing bridge between this dashboard and your equity book — FutureFuel's restart economics and Delek's exposure ride the same headlines that move ZL. One wire, two books.

The Black Sea war regime

Ukrainian export capacity and Russian export taxes/quotas are permanent wheat variables while the war runs; the Matif−CBOT spread and Egyptian/GASC tender results are the honest gauges under the headline noise.

China as scheduled demand

The 25 MMT purchase framework converts China from a price-driven buyer into a politically scheduled one — bullish while kept, binary if broken. Track pace-vs-schedule, not rhetoric.

South American logistics & FX

BRL and ARS drive origination selling (as with coffee); Argentine export-tax politics create episodic supply waves; Brazilian infrastructure keeps compressing its FOB discount — the structural share-shift running under everything, the grains version of Brazil-in-cotton.

Brazil parity cross-link

Sugar–ethanol parity at Brazilian mills sets marginal world sugar supply: when hydrous ethanol is more profitable than sugar (equivalent-ton math), mills shift toward ethanol and sugar supply tightens; when sugar wins, hydrous production falls.   • Early flowering as a stripper, not a saviour — isolated out-of-season blossoms across Southeast Brazil consume tree reserves without setting a uniform crop.   • Colombia's own bad year sourced — FNC 26 projection revised down to 12.5 M bags (vs 14.8 M two years ago) after flowering-rainfall damage; the natural US-tariff substitute origin is not scaling to fill the Brazilian gap. Central-Colombian arabica differentials firming.
  • Rabobank's bear call vs the ground — Rabobank sees arabica falling roughly a third by Q4 2026 on the record 189.7 M crop. The approaching Oct 10 failure trigger is the ground's answer to that call; the debate resolves over the next 18 days.
  • Roaster caution, no panic bid yet — forward coverage at 40–60% of annual needs 6–12 months out. No evidence yet of panic-buy sequences. The roaster bid historically arrives fast once co-op agronomists begin publishing bare-branch photos — one viral image from a reputable co-op can move the market.
  • Tariff workaround talk — rerouting, triangulation, differential games. When the physical trade discusses logistics tricks, watch differentials rather than flat price.

How to listen (no accounts needed)

StoneX's free daily coffee report is the single best listening post — COT detail, spreads, EFP flow, origin-selling colour, in trade language. Safras & Mercado and Cooxupé's harvest/selling-pace updates give the Brazilian producer's revealed behaviour. Reuters' Brazil coffee dispatches, Comunicaffe recaps and co-op president quotes (Expocacer, Cooabriel) carry the ground voice. And uniquely to coffee: the florada is photographed — Brazilian producers flood social media with flowering images every Sep–Nov, a literal visual crowd-census of the next crop that requires no account to browse via news aggregation.

Bias corrections for this market

Opposite polarity to West Africa: Brazilian producers are fully price-exposed and hold inventory, so they have every incentive to talk the crop down — treat producer pessimism like US-farmer crop talk (de-mean it, demand verifiable specifics). The honest tell is behaviour, not words: Safras' selling-pace percentages and Cecafé export volumes reveal what farmers actually believe. With BRL strengthening (~5.12), that behaviour is currently to hold rather than sell — a real signal, not talk. For florada photos, apply the against-interest rule: a beautiful bloom photo is bearish for the poster's own old-crop inventory — pride posting against the book, hence credible. Read the photo stream, not individual photos: aggregate timing, density and uniformity across regions vs the seasonal baseline is the signal no selection bias can fake. Vietnamese farmer chatter is thin in English; export pace vs crop estimates is the readable proxy for their hoarding.
