The reference frame

Not education — the machinery list, so refreshes are mechanical.

The report calendar (the densest in commodities)

Monthly: WASDE (~10-12th) — the referee for everything. Weekly: crop progress & conditions (Mon), export inspections (Mon), export sales (Thu), CFTC COT (Fri). Quarterly: Grain Stocks (the ambush report — largest surprise variance of any USDA release) and, annually, Prospective Plantings (31 Mar) and June Acreage. Daily: USDA flash sales announcements (the "daily reporting system" — 100kt+ sales same-day). South America: CONAB monthly, Rosario & Buenos Aires exchange weeklies. Black Sea: SovEcon/IKAR/UkrAgroConsult.

The hemispheric mirror

The complex never sleeps: US crop made May–Sep, priced into harvest (now); South American crop planted Sep–Dec, made Dec–Feb (soy) and Feb–May (safrinha corn), priced against the US old crop. Every northern autumn, attention hands off to SA planting weather — that handoff is happening this month, into an El Niño whose classic signature (wet southern Brazil/Argentina) is supply-friendly.

The structures you already trade in spirit

Board crush (beans vs meal+oil) — the processing margin, currently policy-inflated on the oil side via biodiesel. Old/new crop spreads (Dec/Jul corn, Nov/Jul beans) — where "this crop vs next crop" trades explicitly, the natural home of the SA-planting view. Wheat class spreads (KC−Chicago protein premium, Minneapolis spring) and Matif−CBOT (the Black Sea stress gauge in EUR). Corn−wheat feed substitution. These are the aggregate positioning structures — legs never in isolation, per your standing rule.

Equity-book cross-links (unique to this dashboard)

Biofuels policy → FutureFuel, Delek: RVO/45Z headlines move soyoil and the biodiesel margin stack — the same alert serves both books. Farm income mood → the Deere ecosystem: the monthly Purdue Ag Economy Barometer is the standing capex-sentiment gauge; record grain prices now = the 2027 equipment/irrigation spending impulse. Ethanol grind → corn demand and the refining-adjacent names.

Standing policy & structural variables

Biofuels: the demand policy lever

US RVO volumes and the 45Z clean-fuel credit set the marginal demand for corn (ethanol) and soyoil (biodiesel/renewable diesel). This is the standing bridge between this dashboard and your equity book — FutureFuel's restart economics and Delek's exposure ride the same headlines that move ZL. One wire, two books.

The Black Sea war regime

Ukrainian export capacity and Russian export taxes/quotas are permanent wheat variables while the war runs; the Matif−CBOT spread and Egyptian/GASC tender results are the honest gauges under the headline noise.

China as scheduled demand

The 25 MMT purchase framework converts China from a price-driven buyer into a politically scheduled one — bullish while kept, binary if broken. Track pace-vs-schedule, not rhetoric.

South American logistics & FX

BRL and ARS drive origination selling (as with coffee); Argentine export-tax politics create episodic supply waves; Brazilian infrastructure keeps compressing its FOB discount — the structural share-shift running under everything, the grains version of Brazil-in-cotton.

Ethanol complex

Note - written 16 Sep 2026 - not auto-updated

Ethanol is ~35–40% of US corn demand. The crush margin drives weekly grind pace — the demand-side swing variable on the corn balance sheet. When margins collapse, grind cuts show up in WASDE usage within 1–2 months and hit the record MM long directly.

Brazil parity cross-link

Sugar–ethanol parity at Brazilian mills sets marginal world sugar supply: when hydrous ethanol is more profitable than sugar (equivalent-ton math), mills shift toward ethanol and sugar supply tightens; when sugar wins, hydrous production falls. This is the mechanism that makes crude → sugar a real transmission. Counterpart annotation now lives in sugar-dashboard.html v1.1 under "The ethanol hinge".
