The market in one page

The permanent structure. Refresh yearly.

Demand is a macro trade wearing a crop's clothes

Unlike cocoa, coffee and sugar — habit goods — cotton demand is apparel demand: cyclical, discretionary, retail-inventory-driven. Mill use swings with global consumer strength and the textile restocking cycle. And cotton competes for every shirt with polyester (~2/3 of world fiber), whose cost tracks oil: crude up = polyester dear = cotton competitive, and vice versa. Cotton therefore carries a genuine crude beta and a consumer-cycle beta stacked together — the most "macro" of your softs.

The supply story of the decade: Brazil over the US

Brazil has become the world's #3 producer and #1 exporter, eclipsing the United States — a second crop (safrinha cotton after soybeans in Mato Grosso), industrial scale, comparable quality, lower cost. US export share fell from ~39% (2016) to ~26% (2023) under trade disputes and this competition. The US remains the benchmark origin for the futures contract; Brazil increasingly sets the physical price. Same structural-share-shift pattern as Ecuador in cocoa — the incumbent's problems are the challenger's business plan.

China: buyer, producer, and policy variable

Largest producer (Xinjiang) and largest importer; its state reserve system makes Chinese stock numbers semi-opaque — hence the trade watches world-less-China stocks as the cleaner tightness gauge. Chinese purchases of US cotton are a trade-politics instrument, switched on and off with negotiations.

The calendar

US: planting Mar–Jun (Prospective Plantings 31 Mar), development through the summer (weekly crop-condition ratings), harvest Sep–Dec — the December contract is the US new-crop benchmark. India/Pakistan: monsoon sowing Jun–Sep, harvest Oct–Feb. Brazil: safrinha planted Jan–Feb after soybeans, harvested Jun–Sep — meaning Brazilian supply lands mid-year, right on top of the US old-crop tail. Australia: water-allocation dependent, harvest Mar–Jun.

El Niño's cotton map

Split verdict again, but a different split than sugar: El Niño typically brings wetter conditions to the US cotton belt (drought relief — bearish via better US yields), drier monsoons to India/Pakistan (bullish), and reduced water allocations in Australia (bullish). Net: mildly supportive ex-US, with the US leg actually working against the bulls.

Contract mechanics worth knowing

The weekly on-call report shows mill purchases priced but not yet fixed against futures — heavy unfixed on-call sales into a rally are forced future buying, cotton's recurring squeeze mechanism (2021–22's run to 155¢ had this engine). ICE certified stocks, the AWP (adjusted world price) and the A-Index round out the plumbing.

The current storylive · 23 Sep 2026

Where we are in the cycle and what the market is arguing about.

The tape is fading a florada it hasn't verified. KCZ26 closed 284.25 ¢/lb on 11 September, a fresh 10-week low and −16.4% over 30 days, on favourable near-term GFS runs for Brazil and Rabobank's call for arabica to fall roughly another third by year-end. Two sessions later Somar reported zero rain in Minas Gerais for the week ended 13 September — a total reversal of the 8.9mm / 127%-of-average priming print of 30 August that set the "flowering-friendly" narrative in the first place. The tape and the ground are moving in opposite directions for the first time this window, and that is the setup the florada playbook was written for.

The tension is in the stocks, not the crop. The record 2026/27 crop has arrived — Brazil finished harvest at a realized ~75 M bags (StoneX/Sucafina/Marex, above USDA's 71.9 M June print) and USDA still projects world output at a record 189.7 M bags (+6%). Yet certified arabica stocks have ground down to ~223,900 bags — the lowest since 1999, undercutting the 226k mark flagged only a week ago. The 50% US tariff on Brazilian coffee is the persistent driver: US buyers substituting Colombia and other origins, exporters holding back Brazilian flow, and pending gradings not building the refill sequence. Robusta certs meanwhile sit near multi-month highs with Vietnam projected at a record 32.5 M bag crop. The two exchanges continue to tell opposite stories — nearby arabica tightness inside an aggregate surplus.

The forward fight is 2027/28, and September has just delivered its first hostile datapoint. The bull case stacks the biennial down-year, a now-verified strong El Niño (ONI +1.80) hitting the Sep–Oct florada, and Espírito Santo heat risk through January bean-filling. The 13 Sep zero-rain print is exactly the shape of the failure trigger — one week is not a verdict, but the moisture debt widens with every dry week, and the 🔴 "no meaningful widespread rains by ~10 October" line is now under three weeks away. Bear counterweights remain live: irrigation resilience (23/24 El Niño cut Brazil only ~8%), the frost tail suppressed by El Niño, Vietnam robusta comfortable, Rabobank's 9.5 M-bag arabica surplus rebuilding stocks first, and the Colombia offset already failing — the FNC now projects Colombia at only 12.5 M bags after climate-driven flowering losses of its own, which tightens rather than loosens the tariff-substitute chain. Positioning has cleaned to +22.1k managed money net long (8 Sep COT), so any conviction move lands on mid-range books.

The synthesis

Even the record year only lifts ending stocks to ~26 M bags — one bad season's worth of buffer, and that buffer already erodes at the origin margin as Colombia's 26 crop is revised down 2+ M bags. The scenario tree is compressing: a rescued florada (widespread October soakings) means two consecutive rebuild years and a price grind back toward the pre-spike range (the June low at ~240 marks that world); a failed florada — the trend as of 22 Sep — means the record crop's surplus is spent the moment it exists, and with certs at 27-year lows the market retraces the ~$3.50–4.00 zone faster than it came down.

Positioning context: arabica MM net long +20,661 (56.3th pctile, Sep 15 COT) — mid-range. The June washout cleared the crowded long; fresh fundamental buying has room. A failed-florada verdict lands on clean positioning — a larger, not smaller, potential price response than if funds were already extended.

Uncertainty note: USDA, CONAB and private forecasters routinely disagree on Brazil by 5+ M bags (different survey methods), and the tariff makes trade-flow data temporarily unreliable as a demand proxy. CONAB's third 2026 survey (September) and the first 27/28 estimates (January) are the anchors to watch; the 11 Sep WASDE left the world balance unchanged from June.

Standing policy & structural variables

US–China trade: the flow switch

Chinese purchases of US cotton are a negotiating instrument — switched on for goodwill, off for leverage. Every trade-talks cycle reprices US export demand directly; China's structural diversification (Brazil, Australia) means each "off" period does permanent share damage. The single biggest headline risk in the market.

US farm economics & policy

Multi-year negative margins have US acreage decisions running on rotation economics and farm-program support rather than optimism; watch farm-bill-adjacent programs for acreage effects. A structurally shrinking US supply base is the slow-burn bull leg under the Brazilian takeover.

Sustainability & traceability

Xinjiang-related import bans (UFLPA) already re-route Chinese-origin textiles; EU traceability rules extend the theme. Effect: bifurcated demand for "compliant" cotton — a quiet premium for US/Brazilian/Australian origin in Western supply chains.

Polyester and oil policy

Anything that structurally moves oil — OPEC policy, the current conflict — is fiber-substitution policy in disguise. The cotton/polyester price ratio is the demand-side gauge worth a monthly glance.

How to listen (no accounts needed)

StoneX's free daily coffee report is the single best listening post — COT detail, spreads, EFP flow, origin-selling colour, in trade language. Safras & Mercado and Cooxupé's harvest/selling-pace updates give the Brazilian producer's revealed behaviour. Reuters' Brazil coffee dispatches, Comunicaffe recaps and co-op president quotes (Expocacer, Cooabriel) carry the ground voice. Somar's weekly Minas rainfall print is the numeric input the trade prices around. And uniquely to coffee: the florada is photographed — Brazilian producers flood social media with flowering images every Sep–Nov, a literal visual crowd-census of the next crop that requires no account to browse via news aggregation.

Bias corrections for this market

Opposite polarity to West Africa: Brazilian producers are fully price-exposed and hold inventory, so they have every incentive to talk the crop down — treat producer pessimism like US-farmer crop talk (de-mean it, demand verifiable specifics). The honest tell is behaviour, not words: Safras' selling-pace percentages and Cecafé export volumes reveal what farmers actually believe. With BRL strengthening (~5.12), that behaviour is currently to hold rather than sell — a real signal, not talk. For florada photos, apply the against-interest rule: a beautiful bloom photo is bearish for the poster's own old-crop inventory — pride posting against the book, hence credible. Read the photo stream, not individual photos: aggregate timing, density and uniformity across regions vs the seasonal baseline is the signal no selection bias can fake. Vietnamese farmer chatter is thin in English; export pace vs crop estimates is the readable proxy for their hoarding.

How this market trades

Positioning first, again: multi-year fund long into a revision-prone tightness story — the sugar setup with a weaker demand floor. Because the same funds hold both, treat sugar+cotton as one correlated crowding exposure; a CTA-style ag washout hits them together, whatever their separate fundamentals do. The cocoa short and the coffee neutrality are the natural other side of that book — the softs complex currently offers a positioning-driven relative-value structure across four markets sharing one weather driver.

Liquidity is decent, gaps are moderate — deeper than cocoa, thinner than sugar. The contract's special mechanics (on-call fixations, certified stocks, AWP) create recurring squeezes at the front; the 2021–22 run to 155¢ is the modern template for what crowded on-calls plus a weather scare can do.

The structure trades

  • Cotton vs crude (polyester parity): the demand-side spread; cotton underperforming crude rallies signals the war premium is fiber-specific fatigue.
  • Dec (US new crop) vs deferred: where US weather trades against the Brazilian mid-year wall.
  • Cross-soft crowding RV: long the clean-positioned El Niño expression (coffee) vs the crowded ones (sugar, cotton) — one driver, opposite pricings, defined by the COT rather than the weather.

The bull case, honestly

  • Ex-China stocks-to-use at decade lows; consumption outran production through the revision cycle.
  • Heat-dome yield damage not yet in the numbers; India's monsoon deficit real.
  • China's smallest crop in 3 years + 6-yr-high consumption = import pull.
  • Crude strength holds the polyester floor; US farm economics shrink the supply base structurally.
  • On-call mechanics can turn any rally reflexive.

The bear case, honestly

  • Record fund long into a forecast that USDA itself has been revising looser.
  • El Niño waters Texas; US acreage +4%; Brazil's 2nd-largest crop ever keeps landing.
  • Apparel demand is cyclical — cotton's demand can break in a downturn in a way food softs' can't.
  • A ceasefire-style crude break removes the polyester floor and the war premium at once (May→June showed the speed).
  • Chinese buying is politics — it can vanish with one negotiation turn.
